## Job Market Paper

**[Don't Try Again: Screening Through Attempt Limits](/files/Billari_Dont_Try_Again.pdf)**

Licensing and civil-service examinations often cap the number of times a candidate may take them. While economists have long studied how tests should be designed to reveal information, I use mechanism design theory to model the attempt limit as a device for screening candidates' skills through the dynamic allocation of testing opportunities. In a two-period model, agents privately know their skill, and low-skilled agents can acquire skills between attempts. An attempt limit grants a single attempt but lets the agent choose when to use it: high-skilled agents take the test at once, whereas low-skilled agents postpone it and acquire skills in the meantime. The limit sorts agents before testing them, but it is incentive compatible only if the test is not too lenient, and it denies high-skilled agents a second chance. I characterize when the attempt limit is the optimal testing policy, first taking the leniency and accuracy of the test as given, then choosing leniency jointly with the allocation of attempts, and finally choosing costly accuracy as well. I show that, consistent with the model, attempt limits on the U.S. bar exam are tightest at intermediate passing scores and rare where the exam is lenient.

## Working Papers

**[Media Capture in Local News Markets](/files/Billari_Media_Capture_Local_News.pdf)**

This paper studies how the revenue structure of local news outlets affects political bias. In a Bayesian persuasion model, a monopolist outlet chooses between advertising and subscription income and may be captured by the incumbent. I show that the relationship between advertising revenues and bias is generally inverted-U shaped: when per-reader advertising revenues are low, as after the entry of online platforms into advertising markets, a further decline raises subscription prices, leads readers to demand more informative coverage, and reduces pro-incumbent bias.

## Work in Progress

**Competition among Licensing Authorities**

How competition among jurisdictions for mobile professionals shapes attempt limits, passing standards, and the value of portable test scores.

**Why Do Attempt Limits Raise Passing Rates? A Quantitative Analysis**

A dynamic model of repeated attempts, calibrated to U.S. bar exam data, that decomposes the effect of attempt limits into the exclusion of candidates who exhaust their attempts and candidates' responses to the limit.

**[Visual Media Bias: Evidence from Computer Vision and Machine Learning](/files/Billari_Visual_Media_Bias.pdf)**

Whether newspapers use less flattering photographs of politicians from the opposing party, measured with computer vision and identified from the moment a candidate becomes the presumptive nominee.
